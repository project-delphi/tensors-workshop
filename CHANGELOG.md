- **The audio stage says what the short-time Fourier transform *is*.** It had taught every step of it -- a frame, the transform of that frame, the hop -- and never said the whole thing is one matrix product, which is the same `A @ B` a reader met in section 06. Three more display equations now do: *one window* defines the frames matrix `𝒳 ∈ ℝ^(N×T)`, whose column `t` is sample `t·H + n` read at a stride and which computes nothing at all; *the transform* defines `ℱ`, the transform written out as a matrix whose row `f` is one sinusoid sampled `N` times; and *the hop* puts them together as `X = ℱ · diag(w) · 𝒳`, with the shapes `(513 × 1024)·(1024 × 465) → 513 × 465` and `n` named as the axis that disappears. The two new objects are script capitals, like the batch scene's `𝒯`, because `F` already counts frequency bins on this page and `T` counts frames.
- **The cost of both routes, in multiply-adds rather than milliseconds.** The product is 244 270 080 of them for the recording the stage ships; a transform per frame is 4 761 600, a factor of 51.3 -- which is why `stft()` and not the matrix product is what the page actually runs, and why the FFT is an algorithm rather than a different transform. A timing would have been the reader's machine; the count is the same number for everyone. `npm test` builds both matrices, multiplies them, and requires the answer to be the one `stft()` gives.
- Pointing at `N`, `n` or `H` now bands what those letters measure, the way `f` and `t` already did: the width of one frame, the half of the bars the transform keeps, and the step between two overlapping windows -- so the overlap is the difference between two brackets on the same picture. And the browser check's equation selectors are scoped to their own section: they were document-global, which was unambiguous while one section carried `freq` and picked the wrong one the moment a second did.
